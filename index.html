Deep Learning · Medical Imaging
Completed March 2026

Breast Cancer Detection — CNN Classifier

A convolutional neural network for classifying breast histopathology images as benign or malignant, with a clinical focus on maximizing recall to minimize missed diagnoses.

Problem
Manual histopathology review is time-consuming, subjective, and dependent on specialist availability — creating bottlenecks in early breast cancer diagnosis.
Approach
Custom CNN trained on the IDC histopathology dataset (224×224px patches), optimized for recall on malignant class — because in oncology, a missed malignant case carries far greater cost than a false alarm.
Impact
A scalable AI-assisted triage tool that flags high-risk tissue patches for clinician review, supporting faster and more consistent diagnostic workflows.

Causal Inference · Cardiology
Expected June 2026

Who Benefits Most from Cardiovascular Risk Interventions?

An uplift modeling project that goes beyond predicting risk to predicting treatment effect — identifying which patients would actually benefit from intervention.

Problem
Standard risk models predict who is at risk, not who responds to treatment — leading to interventions that miss the patients they would help most.
Approach
Uplift modeling using causal inference techniques to estimate individual treatment effects, separating responders from those who would do well either way.
Impact
A precision medicine framework that targets cardiovascular interventions to the patients most likely to benefit — a model for value-based care.

Predicting Drug Shortages Before They Happen

A classification model that flags pharmaceutical products at elevated risk of supply shortage — a real industry problem I've watched unfold from the inside.

ML · Patient Behavior
Capstone Expected August 2026

Medication Adherence Segmentation

A combined supervised and unsupervised learning approach to segment patients by medication adherence patterns.

Problem
Non-adherence costs the US healthcare system over $300B annually, yet most interventions treat all non-adherent patients alike — missing the distinct behavioral patterns that drive different solutions.
Approach
Combined unsupervised clustering to discover adherence archetypes, then supervised classification to assign new patients to the segment most likely to predict their behavior.

Healthcare + AI Philosophy

How I think about building AI in healthcare.

Models in healthcare are not finished when they're accurate. They're finished when they can be trusted, explained, and used responsibly in real clinical and operational workflows. A few principles that guide how I approach this work.

01

Explainable AI is not optional

A model that can't tell a clinician why doesn't belong in a clinical decision. Interpretability isn't a feature — it's a precondition for trust, adoption, and safe deployment in healthcare.

02

Patient-centered, always

The patient is the end user, even when they're invisible from the model. Every architectural choice should be measured against whether it ultimately serves patient outcomes — not algorithmic elegance or organizational convenience.

03

Operational realism over benchmark theater

A model that wins on a test set but fails inside a clinical workflow has failed. Performance on paper means little if it can't be operationalized — monitored, maintained, and trusted by the people who use it daily.

04

AI augments judgment, never replaces it

The most valuable healthcare AI doesn't make decisions for clinicians and operators — it makes their decisions sharper. Decision support is a partnership between the model and the human expertise that contextualizes its output.
